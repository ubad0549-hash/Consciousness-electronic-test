# Definition of consciousness
The definitions should be grounded in information processing and organization so they can be aligned with biological processes that deal with processing experience by trading informational mechanisms.

General definition by Merriam-Webster: The quality or state of being aware, especially of something within oneself.

Vithoulkas and Muresanu define consciousness as "the function of the human mind that receives and processes information, crystallizes it, and then stores it or rejects it..." (2).
  
  First definition: first-person experience that has qualia.
Personal discussion about the first definition
What is first person and what is qualia? Can we turn these terms into mechanisms that the brain or some form of information processing can explain? Is this definition justified enough to make a mechanistic, step-by-step process for Consciousness?
We can define first-person experience and qualia as information processing. The first-person perspective can be described as a type of information processing directed toward a single center, which directly processes inputs and produces outputs. (personal definition to mechanize it). We can define qualia as a meta-understanding of, and direct involvement in, information processing. These definitions are not meant to encapsulate the actual meaning of the words but to provide some ground of inference.
These definitions of first-person and qualia can describe brain processes and different informational systems.
I would argue that these definitions are justified for describing mechanisms, but I believe we need stricter bounds on what qualifies as first-person and qualia.
